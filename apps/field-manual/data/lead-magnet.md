# The CYVX Operator Starter Manual

**Seven systems to build, secure, sell, operate, own, capitalize, and verify modern capability.**

This manual is designed for operators starting with limited money, limited equipment, or limited access—but unwilling to build disconnected demos. Every system follows the same contract:

- Name the real outcome.
- Identify the constraint.
- Execute the smallest complete system.
- Add a control before the dangerous step.
- Verify the result with evidence.
- Record what changed.
- Improve the next cycle.

## How to use this manual

## System 1 — SECURE: Protect the identity that controls everything

Your email, phone number, password manager, cloud accounts, domain, and payment account form a control plane. A compromise in one can become a compromise everywhere.

### Mission

Create a recoverable identity baseline that does not depend on one reused password, one phone number, or one device.

### Actions

1. Secure your primary email first with a unique password or passkey.
2. Enable authenticator-based or hardware-key multi-factor authentication where supported.
3. Save recovery codes offline in a secure location.
4. Add a carrier account PIN or port-out lock.
5. Review active sessions, recovery methods, forwarding rules, and connected apps.
6. Move business-owned systems into business-owned accounts.
7. Remove access that is no longer required.

### Verification

- A new login requires the intended authentication factor.
- Unknown sessions are revoked.
- Recovery can be completed without exposing the secret publicly.
- The domain, cloud, payment, and social accounts have at least two authorized recovery paths.

## System 2 — BUILD: Ship one complete capability

A real build accepts input, applies logic, stores state, returns an output, validates behavior, and survives restart. A polished page without those properties is not a production capability.

### Mission

Ship one mobile-first system with a backend, interface, durable storage, validation, logs, tests, documentation, and a measurable outcome.

### Actions

1. Define one user and one job the system must complete.
2. Write the input, model, action, output, and evidence contract.
3. Build the smallest connected backend and interface.
4. Store state durably and validate every external input.
5. Log actions using timestamps and correlation identifiers.
6. Test success, failure, invalid input, unauthorized access, and restart behavior.
7. Document the run and verification commands.

### Verification

- A new user can complete the target job.
- Data remains after restart.
- Invalid input is rejected safely.
- The system exposes a health check.
- Tests fail when the core behavior is broken.

## System 3 — SELL: Turn a skill into an outcome contract

Buyers do not pay for how difficult your skill feels. They pay to move from an expensive current state to a valuable future state with controlled risk.

### Mission

Create a named fixed-scope offer with a clear buyer, deliverable, deadline, acceptance criteria, price, and next action.

### Actions

1. Pick one buyer with an urgent and observable problem.
2. Describe the current cost of leaving the problem unsolved.
3. Define the exact deliverable and what is excluded.
4. State the deadline and customer responsibilities.
5. Define objective acceptance criteria.
6. Price from delivery economics, risk, value, and margin.
7. Create one honest proof asset and one start action.

### Verification

Ask a qualified buyer to explain the offer back to you. They should be able to state the outcome, timeline, price, evidence, and next step without your help.

## System 4 — OPERATE: Build a one-person company control loop

A one-person business fails when decisions, leads, delivery, money, and evidence are scattered across messages and memory.

### Mission

Create one operating view that identifies the current constraint and the next action across sales, delivery, cash, support, and improvement.

### Actions

1. Create one source of truth for customers, offers, work, invoices, evidence, and next actions.
2. Assign one owner and due date to every active action.
3. Separate proposed, invoiced, paid, fulfilled, and accepted outcomes.
4. Add approval gates for messages, contracts, purchases, payments, and destructive actions.
5. Run a daily constraint review and a weekly evidence review.
6. Close dead work instead of hiding it in a backlog.
7. Record the learning that changes the next cycle.

### Verification

At any moment, you can answer:

- What outcome are we pursuing?
- What is blocking it?
- What is the next action?
- Who owns it?
- When is it due?
- What evidence proves completion?
- What did the last cycle teach us?

## System 5 — OWN: Evaluate assets by control and cash flow

A claimed asset may actually be a liability, a job, a speculative position, or a right you do not control.

### Mission

Evaluate a business, product, property, domain, content library, or digital system using ownership, cash flow, durability, transferability, and risk.

### Actions

1. Identify the actual legal or practical ownership right.
2. List gross inflows and every recurring or hidden outflow.
3. Separate operator labor from asset cash flow.
4. Stress-test customer, supplier, platform, location, and owner concentration.
5. Inspect transfer restrictions, licenses, contracts, maintenance, and liabilities.
6. Define downside, break-even, and recovery scenarios.
7. Name the evidence required before purchase or expansion.

### Verification

You can explain where the money comes from, where it goes, what can break, who controls the system, and what happens when the current operator disappears.

## System 6 — CAPITAL: Control cash before chasing funding

Revenue, profit, bank balance, available cash, and runway are different facts. A business can be profitable and still miss an obligation.

### Mission

Build a weekly cash-control view covering posted cash, committed outflows, receivables, restricted funds, and reserve policy.

### Actions

1. Separate business and personal accounts.
2. Reconcile bank and payment-processor activity.
3. Track opening cash, posted inflows, posted outflows, and closing cash.
4. Add upcoming obligations and realistic collection dates.
5. Reserve for taxes, payroll, refunds, repairs, and operational continuity.
6. Report missing, pending, stale, and partially synced data.
7. Review actual margin after delivery and correct pricing assumptions.

### Verification

The first reported cash number includes its coverage date and connected-account scope. Every material difference between records and bank activity is explained.

## System 7 — WEB3: Connect on-chain actions to off-chain reality

A token, wallet interface, or verified contract does not automatically create safety, legal ownership, or reliable cash flow.

### Mission

Use a reality-first review before signing a blockchain transaction or treating a token as an asset.

### Actions

1. Confirm the chain, contract address, asset, issuer, and jurisdiction.
2. Inspect source verification, upgradeability, owner privileges, fees, pauses, and withdrawal paths.
3. Review audits, disclosed issues, admin-key controls, and post-audit changes.
4. Simulate the transaction and limit approvals.
5. Use separate low-value wallets for experiments.
6. Define what legal or practical right the token represents.
7. Connect on-chain records to off-chain custody, servicing, governance, and evidence.

### Verification

You know exactly what the transaction authorizes, what can be changed later, who controls privileged functions, what you receive, and how failure or dispute is handled.

## The 30-day operator mission

### Week 1 — Secure and define

- Complete the identity baseline.
- Choose one user, problem, and measurable outcome.
- Write the mission card.
- Remove one unnecessary access path.

### Week 2 — Build and verify

- Ship the smallest connected capability.
- Add durable storage, validation, logs, and tests.
- Capture proof of the working outcome.
- Document the run and verification command.

### Week 3 — Sell and operate

- Package the capability as a named offer.
- Create one honest proof asset.
- Create the sales and delivery pipeline.
- Measure response time, qualification, proposals, collected revenue, and acceptance.

### Week 4 — Own, capitalize, and improve

- Separate revenue from collected cash.
- Calculate actual delivery margin.
- Identify the asset created by the work.
- Record the largest constraint and next improvement.
- Decide what should become a kit, membership asset, software feature, or recurring service.

## Final operating review

- What real outcome was completed?
- What evidence proves it?
- What failed?
- What was the primary constraint?
- What action changed the outcome most?
- What should be automated?
- What must remain approval-gated?
- What reusable asset now exists?
- What can generate recurring value?
- What will the next cycle improve?

## Important limitations

This manual is educational. It does not provide individualized legal, tax, accounting, cybersecurity, investment, lending, insurance, or financial advice. Provider interfaces, laws, security conditions, and market conditions change. Verify current requirements and use qualified professionals for consequential decisions.
